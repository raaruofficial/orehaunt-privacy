1. Definitions

2. Scope

This policy explains what HAUNT does and does not collect when you use it, what stays on your device, what leaves your device, and what will change if and when the companion backend is hosted. Plain language throughout — if anything here is unclear, email raaruofficial+support@gmail.com.

3. Data we collect

3.1 No accounts, and no server-side collection today

  • HAUNT has no account sign-up and no login of its own in the current build.
  • The companion backend (haunt-backend) is built but not hosted, and the shipped app is configured with PAYMENTS.backend = null. That means, today:
  • the app does not send your purchases, chat messages, or browsing in the app to a HAUNT server — there is no HAUNT server receiving them;
  • payment verification happens on your device (see §5);
  • entitlements are stored only in your browser / WebView storage (see §3.2).
  • Section 7 describes, as forward-looking statements only, what changes when the backend is hosted.
  • When you sign in with Connect with Seeker, the connection runs over a third-party relay service that can observe your wallet address, the fact that you are connecting to HAUNT, and when; your wallet approves every sign-in, and nothing in this statement changes what HAUNT itself collects (nothing) or where your pass lives (only on your device).

The app stores the following in your browser's / WebView's local storage (and, for an in-flight payment, session storage) on your own device. This data is not transmitted to us in the current build.

3.3 Chat today is local-only

Chat in the current build is a local preview: messages are stored only in haunt_chat_v1 on your device, are not sent to a HAUNT server, and are not visible to other users. The app labels it as a local preview in its own UI. A live, multi-user chat transport is a future step (see §7).

3.4 The intel dataset is bundled, not tracked

The miner leaderboard, dossiers, round explorer and replays shown in the app come from a dataset bundled into the app file at build time (settled ORE round history collected from public on-chain data by the project's data pipeline). Displaying it does not require the app to send your identity anywhere.

3.5 What we do not collect

  • No private keys, no seed phrases — ever. Payments are approved by you in your own wallet. The app creates a Solana Pay payment link / request; it never sees or holds your keys.
  • No sale of personal data. There is no server-side personal data to sell in the current build.
  • No third-party advertising SDKs and no advertising identifiers are described in, or were found in, the app source used for this draft. [Owner to confirm before publication that the final store build adds none.]

4. How your data is used

Because there is no server-side collection today, use is local and functional only:

  • To unlock what you bought: the app reads haunt_purchases on your device to decide which premium surfaces to open, and for how long.
  • To remember your place: onboarding, splash and chat-note flags stop the app nagging you about things you have already seen.
  • To run local features: your companion state and your local chat live where you created them — on your device.

5. Wallet, payments, and third-party calls

5.1 Non-custodial by design

HAUNT is non-custodial: your funds stay in your own wallet until you approve a payment in that wallet. We cannot move your funds and we never hold your keys.

5.2 Payments are public on-chain records — by nature, not by our choice

Passes are paid in SOL, ORE or SKR to the app's treasury wallet:

FRShnxX7vi64gtBoA6JPmH9MBuVqtheEQaxA1D17wKdP

When you pay:

  • Your wallet sends a real Solana transaction to that treasury. Solana transactions are public: anyone can see the sending wallet, the receiving wallet, the asset, the amount and the time on a public block explorer. Buying a pass therefore creates a public on-chain record linking your paying wallet to the treasury. We cannot make that record private, and this policy does not pretend otherwise.
  • The quoted token amount is priced from a US-dollar tier price using the Jupiter Price API (see §5.3).
  • In the current build, verification happens on your device: the app reads the public Solana chain (see §5.3), looks for a recent transaction to the treasury, and checks that the treasury's balance increased by at least 99% of the quoted amount for that payment. If it matches, the app writes your pass into haunt_purchases on your device.

5.3 Third-party services the app calls directly from your device

These calls go from your device to the service named — they are not routed through a HAUNT server in the current build:

Each of those services is run by a third party under its own privacy terms. HAUNT does not control them.

5.4 What a price or RPC failure means for your privacy

Nothing extra is collected on failure. If a price cannot be fetched, the app refuses to invent a rate; if verification cannot reach the network, no pass is written.

6. Data retention

  • On-device data (§3.2) stays on your device until you clear it, the pass it records expires (expired records may remain in storage until overwritten or cleared), or you uninstall / clear site data.
  • On-chain payment records (§5.2) are permanent public blockchain records. We cannot delete, edit or expire them, and no request to us can change that.
  • Because there is no server-side collection today, there is no HAUNT server database of your activity to request deletion from in the current build. If the backend is hosted later, §7 applies and this policy will be updated before that change goes live.

7. Forward-looking: what changes when the backend is hosted

This section is forward-looking. None of the following is live today. It is included so the change is disclosed before it happens, not after. This policy will be updated — and the effective date changed — before the backend configuration is switched on in a shipped build.

The built-but-unhosted backend (haunt-backend, SQLite, described in its own README) is designed to:

  • issue payment quotes (POST /v1/pay/quote) and verify payments server-side (POST /v1/pay/verify), binding the resulting entitlement to the wallet that actually paid, as read from the payment transaction — not to a wallet the client merely claims;
  • keep an entitlement ledger, a rolling-24-hour ANOMALY slot ledger (which, for that tier, records a wallet and a source IP for holds, and rate-limits quote attempts by IP), and a Founder Lifetime claim ledger keyed by the paying wallet;
  • offer .skr wallet authentication by signed challenge (/v1/auth/challenge, /v1/auth/verify), which is also the identity rail a future live chat transport would use.
  • While its treasury is unconfigured, that backend refuses settlement by construction (quotes and verification return payments_not_configured / 503).

When that backend is hosted and the app is pointed at it, the data in those ledgers — paying wallet, tier, timestamps, and for ANOMALY holds the source IP — will be collected server-side for the purposes above. 8. Security

On-device storage inherits the security of your device and browser / WebView: anyone with unlocked access to your device can read what the app has stored there, including your local pass records and local chat. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
